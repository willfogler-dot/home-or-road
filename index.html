Assumptions

Your numbers

Household sections take a column for each of you and add them up. Market defaults reflect late September 2026: mortgage rates just crossed 7%, Denver home prices are flat, rents are soft.

How the model worksCash flow rules, the freedom point, rates, uncertainty

Monthly, January 2027 on. Pay and retirement contributions rise with your raise; spending, insurance and road costs with inflation; rent at its own rate. Before a van trip you stay month to month in the townhome; otherwise you rent a house with a yard until you buy.

Where money goes. Brokerage deposits go to brokerage. Take-home pays the bills; cash above your target is invested. Shortfalls come from cash, then brokerage. Retirement is never withdrawn. Taxes on brokerage gains aren't modeled.

Buying. Price grows with home values from today. The mortgage rate is whatever the chosen path says in that month: a straight line from today's rate to the path's 2029 level, flat after. If rates later fall a full point below your loan, you refinance (1.5% of the balance). “When ready” plans buy in the first month you'd keep your cash cushion after closing and housing stays under your share of gross pay.

Van year. No pay or retirement contributions on the road or during the job search. After that, pay restarts at the share you set for the plan's path back. Part-time plans add online income that ramps up from the start of the trip.

Freedom point. The first month your cash, brokerage and retirement could cover a lean life (lean spending plus your housing then) at the safe withdrawal rate. Retirement money is locked until 59½, so an early freedom point needs a taxable bridge; treat it as a direction, not a date to quit on.

Uncertainty. 300 randomized paths: stock returns average your assumption with 16% annual volatility, home values with 6%. Rates follow the selected path.
